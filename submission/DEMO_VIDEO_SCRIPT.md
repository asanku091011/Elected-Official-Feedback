# CivLink demo video script

Target length: **2 minutes 30 seconds**. Record at 1080p, landscape or portrait, with clear
narration. Use the live web app in a desktop browser for the official side and a phone-sized window
for the resident side. No copyrighted music. Replace every `[bracketed]` note with your own details.

The portal requires a YouTube or Vimeo link. Keep the video unlisted/public but accessible to judges.

---

## 0:00–0:15 — The hook

**Screen:** home screen, slowly scrolling.

**Narration:**
> “When something goes wrong in my neighborhood, most people send a complaint to the wrong office and
> never hear back. I built CivLink to fix the routing — and then to show what the right office could
> actually do with it.”

## 0:15–0:45 — The resident experience

**Screen:** tap **Find where my issue goes** → pick **Roads & traffic** → show the office, why, what
it can do, and what it cannot.

**Narration:**
> “I start with the issue, not the office. CivLink names the office with the authority to act, says
> what it can't do, and helps me write a message staff will read. It saves a copy on my device with a
> reference number and opens the office's own contact page. Nothing is sent for me.”

**Screen:** open the **Updates** feed; show a sample announcement and a poll.

> “Residents also get updates and neutral polls for their area. This feed is sample data — the app
> labels it everywhere, because nothing here pretends to come from a real office.”

## 0:45–1:45 — The Official Portal (the main demo)

**Screen:** home → **Open the Official Portal** → sign in as **Sample staff account**. Point at the
“Prototype with sample data” banner.

**Narration:**
> “The part I'm proudest of is the office side. It's a prototype with sample organizations only, and
> it says so on every screen. There's no real sign-in here, but the flow is the one a county would
> need.”

**Screen:** **Polls → New poll.** Type the question and two options. Pick **Loudoun County**. Tick
each neutrality check. Point at the automatic wording hints. Tap **Publish to residents**.

> “Before a poll can publish, the official has to complete a neutrality checklist. CivLink also flags
> campaign wording and exclamation points. Only a verified account can publish, and a resident can
> respond once.”

**Screen:** sign out → **Updates** → open the new poll → vote → show the thanks message and results.

**Screen:** portal → **Polls → Results** → show bars, turnout by locality, then **Export results as
CSV**.

> “Staff see totals and turnout by area — never who voted for what. The results unlock at five
> responses, and the CSV export contains aggregates only.”

**Screen:** **Announcements → New announcement** (optional, 10 seconds) → publish → show it in the
feed.

## 1:45–2:05 — How it works

**Screen:** the **How it works** page with the four-step diagram.

**Narration:**
> “Four steps: an official writes it, CivLink checks it, residents get it in the in-app feed, and
> they read, vote, or reply. The in-app feed is the channel that works today. Push and email are
> labeled not built — I didn't want to claim something I hadn't finished.”

## 2:05–2:20 — Jurisdictions, stated accurately

**Screen:** **Your area** page; point at Loudoun (In VA-10), Fairfax (Part of this county is in
VA-10), and Arlington (Outside VA-10 (VA-8). Supported as an expansion area).

**Narration:**
> “The pilot is Virginia's 10th. The current map still applies after the 2026 redistricting fight, so
> only parts of Fairfax and Prince William are in the district, and Arlington is in the 8th. I'd
> rather label that honestly than overstate the map.”

## 2:20–2:30 — What I learned and what's next

**Screen:** home, or a slide with the logo and the live link/QR.

**Narration:**
> “I learned to model [what you actually built in code, for example: separate sample demo data from
> real state, and test poll rules as plain functions]. Next I'd add a real server, verified official
> accounts, and human moderation, then test it with a real office. CivLink is at
> [your live link].”

## Recording checklist

- [ ] Live URL is deployed and opens on a phone and a desktop browser.
- [ ] Demo data is reset before recording, so the seeded sample content is predictable.
- [ ] No browser tabs, bookmarks, notifications, or personal information visible.
- [ ] Text is readable at 1080p; zoom the browser if needed.
- [ ] Narration is audible and matches what is on screen.
- [ ] Say the words “sample data” and “prototype” at least once.
- [ ] Do not say the app is affiliated with any office, and do not claim push, email, or SMS.
- [ ] End with the live URL and QR code on screen for at least three seconds.
- [ ] No copyrighted music or third-party footage.
